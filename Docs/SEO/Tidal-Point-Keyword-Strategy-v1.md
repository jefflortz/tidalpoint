# Tidal Point Partners Keyword Strategy

**Version:** 1.0

**Date:** August 2026

**Status:** Approved working strategy

**Primary market:** Privately held businesses in Southeastern New England

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Tidal Point Partners should use search language across the website, articles, local landing pages, social distribution, and future marketing programs.

It is intended to prevent three common problems:

1. Optimizing only for Tidal Point's differentiated language while overlooking the phrases prospective clients already recognize.
2. Producing overlapping pages that compete for the same search intent.
3. Adding location names or keywords mechanically in ways that weaken the brand and the quality of the writing.

This is a strategic framework, not a fixed list of exact-match phrases. Search Console, Google Business Profile, client conversations, and qualified-lead data should refine it over time.

## 2. Strategic Position

### Core positioning

Tidal Point provides experienced operating partnership to established privately held businesses at pivotal moments. One experienced operator works alongside owners and leadership teams, supported by a network of specialists when defined expertise or execution capacity is required.

### Search-language principle

**Operating Partner is the differentiated brand position. Familiar service and problem language provides the path to discovery.**

Prospective clients may not begin by searching for an Operating Partner. They are more likely to describe:

- the problem they are experiencing;
- the decision or transition they face;
- the kind of help they believe they need; or
- a familiar category such as business advisor, strategic advisor, leadership advisor, or operations consultant.

The site should meet prospects in that language, then teach them why the Operating Partner model is different.

### Terms to use carefully

- **Consultant / management consultant:** Useful as supporting discovery language, but not the lead brand identity. Tidal Point stays involved in judgment, alignment, and execution rather than delivering a recommendation and leaving.
- **Coach / business coach / executive coach:** May describe part of the experience, but is too narrow and can understate operating accountability. Use only where the intent genuinely concerns leadership development or decision support.
- **Fractional executive:** Adjacent but not equivalent. Use in comparative or explanatory content rather than as the primary service description.
- **PE-backed:** Jeff's experience is valuable proof, but PE-backed companies are not currently a primary target segment. Do not position the firm principally around this audience.
- **Executive:** Use selectively. Leaders, managers, leadership team, and management team are more inclusive of the businesses Tidal Point serves.

## 7. On-Page Optimization Standards

### Titles

- Write for the searcher's decision, not for a keyword-density target.
- Keep the complete rendered title concise enough to communicate its subject clearly.
- Account for the automatic brand suffix in Sanity validation.
- Preserve expressive editorial headlines on the page when a shorter SEO title is useful.
- Avoid using identical lead phrases across multiple pages.

### Descriptions

- Describe the problem, audience, and value of the page.
- Use the primary concept naturally once where possible.
- Treat the description as persuasive search-result copy, not a keyword list.
- Maintain unique descriptions for every indexable page.

### Headings and body copy

- One clear H1 per page.
- Use H2s to answer recognizable questions and organize the reader's decision process.
- Include related terminology naturally rather than repeating an exact phrase.
- Explain what Tidal Point means by Operating Partner on pages where unfamiliar visitors may enter.
- Use specific examples, observable symptoms, decision frameworks, and lived operating experience.

### Internal links

- Link from broad pages to the most relevant pillar content.
- Link pillar articles back to the appropriate service and contact pages.
- Use anchors that describe the destination.
- Avoid relying on “learn more” as anchor text.
- Every indexable page should have at least one crawlable incoming internal link.

### Local references

- Put the strongest geographic signals on the location and contact pages.
- Use regional references on other pages only where they help the reader understand fit or availability.
- Do not repeat a list of municipalities on every page.
- Do not create location pages by replacing place names in a common template.

## 8. Editorial Strategy

### Voice

Content should sound like an experienced operator reflecting on difficult problems—not a detached corporate consultant or an SEO publisher.

The preferred pattern is:

1. Start with a situation an owner or leadership team will recognize.
2. Acknowledge that the problem is difficult and often has more than one cause.
3. Add a light first-person perspective drawn from operating experience.
4. Introduce useful evidence, contemporary sources, relatable cases, or short quotations.
5. Provide a diagnostic framework rather than a simplistic prescription.
6. End with a useful next question or conversation, not a hard sales pitch.

### Content requirements

- Generally 1,000–1,500 words for major articles when the subject warrants it.
- Original point of view and clear practical judgment.
- Relatable examples from established or owner-led businesses.
- Management books and credible published articles as suggested reading.
- Avoid dependence on major consulting-firm reports.
- Use diagrams, quotations, pullouts, or short lists to break up long text.
- Do not guarantee outcomes or present complex business problems as easy fixes.

### Supporting content sequence

After the four pillar articles, prioritize content that deepens them rather than opening many unrelated categories:

1. Operating Partner category education
2. Owner decisions and delegation
3. Operating rhythm and decision rights
4. Leadership-team operating conditions
5. Growth diagnosis and commercial execution
6. Succession and next-generation leadership
7. Acquisition integration and pivotal transitions

## 9. Local SEO Strategy

The initial local objective is to establish Tidal Point as a credible regional operating and business advisory firm, not to rank a collection of thin city pages.

### Current foundation

- Plymouth location
- Southeastern New England service area
- Google Business Profile
- Consistent phone, email, and regional references
- A substantive Southeastern New England landing page

### Near-term priorities

1. Link the regional landing page from the footer, homepage, About, and Who We Serve pages.
2. Keep Google Business Profile services, description, imagery, hours, and URLs current.
3. Establish a legitimate review-acquisition process.
4. Build citations and relationships through relevant chambers, associations, owner networks, professional groups, and regional publications.
5. Add regional proof as it becomes available: client stories, speaking, sponsorships, events, partnerships, or testimonials.
6. Monitor Search Console and GBP data for geographic demand.

### Criteria for a new location page

A narrower location page should only be created when all of the following are true:

- The geography represents a genuine market Tidal Point serves.
- Search Console, GBP, referral, or pipeline data indicates meaningful interest.
- The page can contain unique local context and proof.
- The intent is not already adequately served by the regional page.
- Tidal Point can maintain the page as the business evolves.

## 10. Sanity SEO Fields and Governance

Every indexable page or article should support:

- Search title
- Meta description
- Canonical URL
- Index/noindex control
- Open Graph title
- Open Graph description
- Social image
- Social-image alt text
- Publication date where relevant
- Modified date where relevant
- Primary topic
- Supporting topics
- Geographic relevance when appropriate

### Editorial validation

- Flag missing titles, descriptions, images, and alt text.
- Show the complete rendered search title including any brand suffix.
- Prevent accidental indexing of drafts, prototypes, and utility pages.
- Keep keywords as editorial planning metadata; do not emit a `meta keywords` tag.
- Do not allow multiple pages to claim the same primary intent without an explicit strategic reason.

## 11. Measurement and Review

### Baseline metrics

- Indexed pages
- Sitemap status
- Search impressions
- Organic clicks
- Branded versus non-branded queries
- Average position by topic and landing page
- Location-page impressions and clicks
- Article impressions and clicks
- Organic contact conversions
- Google Business Profile calls, website visits, and direction requests
- Referring domains
- Review count and quality
- Core Web Vitals

### Review cadence

**Monthly during the first six months**

- Review queries gaining impressions.
- Identify pages with impressions but weak click-through rates.
- Check whether Google rewrites titles or descriptions.
- Review queries for language prospects use instead of “Operating Partner.”
- Track geographic query and visitor patterns.
- Record qualified leads by source, problem, role, industry, and geography.

**Quarterly thereafter**

- Revisit the page-to-keyword map.
- Consolidate overlapping content.
- Update pillar articles with new experience, sources, examples, and internal links.
- Decide whether evidence supports a new service, topic, or location page.

## 12. Decisions and Guardrails

1. **Operating Partner remains the principal brand position.**
2. **Business advisor and related familiar terms support discovery; they do not replace the brand.**
3. **The audience includes owners and leadership teams, not owners alone.**
4. **PE-backed companies are not a primary target segment at this stage.**
5. **Southeastern New England is the principal geographic position.**
6. **Local relevance comes from genuine connection and proof, not keyword repetition.**
7. **Each page owns one primary search intent.**
8. **Content quality, judgment, and credibility take precedence over keyword density.**
9. **No additional location pages should be created without evidence and unique content.**
10. **Search Console and qualified pipeline data will refine this strategy.**
